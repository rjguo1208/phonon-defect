Toy 2: honeycomb lattice

A second toy model with two atoms per cell, like hexagonal boron nitride, and displacements in \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\). Updated 2026-10-02.

Status: the host, the light and heavy substitutions and the chain built from the Bloch modes are validated against exact supercells, on a non-polar and on a polar host; the spectral function at a finite concentration is checked against random configurations.

Why a second toy

The first toy validates the upfolding on a square lattice with one scalar displacement per site. It does not test what the first-principles calculation needs:

  • tensor force constants, \(3\times3\) blocks between atoms;
  • two atoms per cell and six branches, with gaps between them;
  • a flexural branch \(\omega_{\mathrm{ZA}}\propto q^2\), which exists only if the force constants obey the rotational sum rules;
  • a periodic host sampled on a \(\mathbf q\)-mesh, as from DFPT, from which the chain can be built in reciprocal space.

Model

A honeycomb lattice with bond length 1, lattice vectors \(\mathbf a_1=(\sqrt3,0)\) and \(\mathbf a_2=(\sqrt3/2,3/2)\), atom A at the origin and atom B at \((0,1)\). Each atom moves in three directions. The harmonic energy is

\[E=\frac12\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}k_1\big[\hat{\mathbf e}_{ij}\cdot(\mathbf u_j-\mathbf u_i)\big]^2+\frac12\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle}k_2\big[\hat{\mathbf e}_{ij}\cdot(\mathbf u_j-\mathbf u_i)\big]^2+\frac12\sum_i\kappa\Big(u^z_i-\frac13\sum_{j\in\mathrm{nn}(i)}u^z_j\Big)^2,\]

with central springs on the nearest-neighbour A–B bonds (\(k_1\)) and on the second-neighbour A–A and B–B bonds (\(k_2\)), and a bending term for every atom that measures how far it sits above or below the plane of its three neighbours. The parameters are \(m_A=1\), \(m_B=1.3\) (close to the boron-to-nitrogen mass ratio), \(k_1=1\), \(k_2=0.2\) and \(\kappa=0.3\), chosen so that the ratio of the ZO to the TO frequency at \(\Gamma\) is 0.63, close to that of h-BN.

  • Sum rules hold exactly. Every term is unchanged by the three rigid translations and the three rigid rotations, so the acoustic and the rotational sum rules hold term by term.
  • The flexural branch is quadratic. A displacement \(u^z\) that is linear in position costs no bending energy, because the three neighbours of an atom are centred on it.
  • In-plane and out-of-plane motion decouple. The mirror plane \(z\to-z\) splits the \(6\times6\) dynamical matrix into a \(4\times4\) in-plane block and a \(2\times2\) out-of-plane block.
  • Defects. A substitution changes the mass of one atom and multiplies every energy term that contains it by a common factor, so the sum rules still hold exactly and need not be imposed afterwards, unlike force constants spliced from a supercell.

Checks of the host

tests/test_honeycomb.py checks the host in six tests:

  • every energy term annihilates the six rigid motions to \(10^{-14}\) (acoustic and rotational sum rules);
  • \(D(\mathbf q)\) is Hermitian, its in-plane and out-of-plane blocks do not couple, the two phase conventions give the same eigenvalues, and there are exactly three zero frequencies at \(\Gamma\);
  • \(\omega_{\mathrm{ZA}}/q^2\) and \(\omega_{\mathrm{TA}}/q\) are constant for \(q\) from 0.002 to 0.008, to \(10^{-3}\) and \(10^{-4}\);
  • no negative \(\omega^2\) on a \(30\times30\) grid;
  • the spectrum of a periodic \(5\times5\) supercell equals the Bloch spectrum on the \(5\times5\) grid to \(10^{-12}\);
  • a substitution keeps exactly three zero modes and the uniform translations.

Defects

Both defects replace an A atom, with the same numbers as in the first toy: a light defect, \(M'=0.3\) with every energy term that contains it multiplied by 1.3, and a heavy defect, \(M'=8\) with the factor 0.6.

  • Light defect: an in-plane local mode (a doublet) at 3.1551, far above the host top 1.862; an out-of-plane mode at 1.4543 inside the gap 1.360–1.549, with 79 % of its weight on the defect; and a shallow in-plane mode at 1.3773, 0.017 above the top of LA, which has no amplitude on the defect site itself and lives on the neighbours.
  • Heavy defect: an in-plane resonance near 0.26 and a flexural (out-of-plane) resonance near 0.05, both inside the host bands.

Cluster and chain from the Bloch modes

The cluster is the defect and the atoms within two bonds, 10 atoms and 30 coordinates. It contains every term that the substitution changes, and the code asserts that the bath block and the coupling block equal those of the host. The coupling \(\mathcal D_{bc}\) has rank 27: the three coordinates of the defect itself do not couple to the bath.

The chain is built from the host modes of an \(L\times L\) \(\mathbf q\)-mesh, without a real-space bath matrix. With the eigenvectors \(\varepsilon_\nu(\mathbf q)\) of the cell-gauge \(D(\mathbf q)\), a cluster coordinate \(c\) (atom \(s_c\) in cell \(\mathbf R_c\), direction \(\alpha_c\)) has the components

\[\langle\mathbf q\nu|e_c\rangle=\frac1L\,e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_c}\,\varepsilon^{*}_{s_c\alpha_c,\nu}(\mathbf q),\]

and block Lanczos on \(\mathrm{diag}(\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu})\) started from them gives the cluster block first (equal to \(W^\dagger\mathcal D^0_{cc}W\) to \(10^{-14}\)) and the chain of the bath after it. This is the reciprocal-space route planned for MoS₂, where DFPT gives the modes on a \(\mathbf q\)-mesh.

  • Supercell gate. On the same mesh the chain terminates (17 blocks for \(8\times8\), 33 for \(12\times12\)) and the upfolded matrix reproduces the defect supercell to \(10^{-12}\) for the host and both defects; the real-space bath chain gives the same result.
  • One chain for every defect. The chain depends only on the host; each defect changes only the \(30\times30\) cluster block.

Results on a 64 × 64 mesh

The chain is built from the \(6\times64^2=24\,576\) modes of a \(64\times64\) mesh, 240 blocks of width 27 (26 minutes on 8 cores, dominated by the full reorthogonalization). The reference is the exact \(64\times64\) defect supercell (sparse direct solves), \(\eta=0.02\).

  • The upfolded local densities of states pass through every exact point, for both polarizations and both defects (Figure 2).
  • The error of the defect-site Green function falls exponentially, to \(5\times10^{-6}\) (light, in-plane), \(6\times10^{-7}\) (light, out-of-plane), \(2\times10^{-6}\) (heavy, in-plane) and \(1\times10^{-7}\) (heavy, out-of-plane) with 240 blocks. The out-of-plane sector converges faster because its band is narrower (top 1.03 against 1.86), as the rate \(\approx4\eta/\omega_{\max}\) per block of the first toy predicts; the flexural resonance at 0.05 sets no extra difficulty.
  • The deep bound states converge like the local mode of the first toy, by about a factor of 100 per block, to the double-precision floor at six blocks: the in-plane local mode \(3.15507807\) and the out-of-plane mode in the gap \(1.45431031\).
  • The shallow in-plane gap mode, \(1.37728296\), converges like the band edge it sits next to: errors \(3.7\times10^{-4}\), \(5.1\times10^{-5}\) and \(1.9\times10^{-8}\) at 20, 40 and 80 blocks.

Spectral function at a finite concentration

With substitutions on randomly chosen A sites, \(n_d\) per cell, the configuration-averaged displacement Green function follows from the T-matrix of one defect, as for the first toy, now as \(6\times6\) matrices:

\[\Sigma_{st}(\mathbf q,z)=n_d\sum_{a\in s,\;b\in t}e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf R_a-\mathbf R_b)}\,T_{ab}(z),\qquad T=(1-Vg^0)^{-1}V,\qquad V(z)=\Delta\Phi-z\,\Delta M,\]

with \(a\), \(b\) the atoms of the cluster (where \(\Delta\Phi\) acts), \(\mathbf R_a\) their cells, and \(g^0\) the displacement Green function of the host from the Bloch modes of a \(384\times384\) mesh. In displacement coordinates a mass change stays local even when the host has long-range forces. The averaged Green function is \([g^0(\mathbf q)^{-1}-\Sigma(\mathbf q)]^{-1}\) (Dyson equation) where the host has modes, and \(g^0+g^0\Sigma g^0\) (first order) where it has none, in the gap 1.360–1.549 and above 1.862: there \(g^0\) is regular, and resumming near the poles of \(T\) would shift the bound states of the defects by an artificial level repulsion. Figure 4 plots \(A(\mathbf q,\omega)=-(2\omega/\pi)\,\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{Tr}\,[M^{1/2}\langle g\rangle M^{1/2}]\) with the host masses, so that every host branch has weight 1. The sign of the Bloch phase in \(\Sigma\) must match that of the dynamical matrix, \(D_{st}(\mathbf q)=\sum_{\mathbf R}D(0s,\mathbf Rt)\,e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R}\): the lattice has no inversion centre, so \(\Sigma(-\mathbf q)=\Sigma(\mathbf q)^{T}\neq\Sigma(\mathbf q)\). The wrong sign leaves \(\Gamma\) and M unchanged (\(-\mathrm M\equiv\mathrm M\)) but moves the flexural branch, whose \(\omega^2\propto q^4\), by tens of per cent.

  • Light defect: the host branches barely change. The bound states of the defects form two flat bands at the frequencies of one defect: the out-of-plane modes at 1.454 inside the gap and the in-plane local modes at 3.155. Their weight depends on \(\mathbf q\): integrated over the gap, the out-of-plane weight is 0.105 near \(\Gamma\), 0.044 at M and 0.040 at K; integrated over 2.6–3.4, the in-plane weight of the local band is 0.110, 0.138 and 0.141.
  • Heavy defect: at long wavelengths the in-plane acoustic branches soften by 3–5 %, where the added mass alone (6 % per cell) would give 3 %. They cross the in-plane resonance at 0.265, LA near \(q=0.28\) and TA near \(q=0.62\) (right panel): below the crossing they are pushed down, above it up (TA 0.226 against 0.236 at \(q=0.55\), 0.336 against 0.333 at \(q=0.8\)), and they are broadest at the crossing, where the full width of the TA peak grows from 0.022, set by \(\eta\), to 0.058. The flexural branch crosses the flexural resonance at \(q=0.65\) without a visible splitting and stays on its host line within the broadening; its width grows only to 0.027.
  • Checks. On the \(64\times64\) mesh the T-matrix reproduces the defect-site densities of states of the upfolded matrix (Figure 2) to \(3\times10^{-5}\)–\(6\times10^{-5}\). For one defect averaged over all its positions on a torus, \(g^0+g^0\Sigma g^0\) with \(n_d=1/N\) is exact; a test checks it to \(10^{-10}\) away from \(\Gamma\) and M, which fixes the sign of the phase. A brute-force average (examples/honeycomb_spectral_check.py: 10 random configurations of 26 defects on a \(36\times36\) torus, diagonalized exactly and unfolded onto Bloch waves, \(\eta=0.02\)) puts the local band of the light defect at 3.155 at \(\Gamma\), M and K and its gap band at 1.455 at \(\Gamma\), as the rule does, while the Dyson equation used everywhere puts them at 3.225–3.235 and 1.495. Between 1.30 and 1.60 the rule is within 4 % of the brute force (median over the path, \(L^1\) distance), the Dyson equation 21 %; below 0.5, on the acoustic branches, the rule is within 0.1 % (light) and 0.5 % (heavy) and the first-order average alone 2.4 % (heavy); on the host ridges the deviation is 0.1 % and 0.2 %.

Polar switch

Hexagonal BN is polar. An option adds in-plane Born charges \(\pm Z\) (A positive, B negative, so they sum to zero) and the dipole–dipole interaction through the screened two-dimensional Coulomb kernel,

\[D^{\mathrm{LR}}_{s\alpha,t\beta}(\mathbf q)=\frac{2\pi Z_sZ_t}{A\sqrt{m_sm_t}}\sum_{\mathbf G}\frac{k_\alpha k_\beta}{|\mathbf k|\,(1+r_{\mathrm{eff}}|\mathbf k|)}\,e^{-|\mathbf k|^2/4\lambda^2}\,e^{-i\mathbf G\cdot(\boldsymbol\tau_t-\boldsymbol\tau_s)},\qquad\mathbf k=\mathbf q+\mathbf G,\]

with \(Z=0.48\), \(r_{\mathrm{eff}}=2\) and Gaussian dipoles of width \(1/\lambda\), \(\lambda=0.7\); the \(\mathbf q=0\) row sums are removed on site (acoustic sum rule). In two dimensions LO and TO stay degenerate at \(\Gamma\) (1.6290) and split linearly away from it, \(\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}-\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}\approx0.302\,|\mathbf q|\); the out-of-plane modes are untouched.

  • A consequence for the chain. With long-range forces the bath couples to the defect atom itself, so in mass-weighted coordinates \(\mathcal D_{bc}\) changes with the defect mass even when the force constants do not. The chain stays a host property: only the columns of \(B_0\) scale, by \(\sqrt{m_{\mathrm{host}}/m_{\mathrm{defect}}}\), which is exact.
  • Tests. Three zero modes and LO = TO at \(\Gamma\), linear splitting, mirror symmetry and stability over the zone; the spectrum of a polar \(5\times5\) torus equals the Bloch spectrum to \(10^{-14}\); on the \(8\times8\) polar defect supercell the scaled coupling reproduces the exact result to \(10^{-9}\) and the unscaled one does not.
  • The polar term changes the host only near \(\Gamma\): the LO–TO splitting starts at \(0.302\,|\mathbf q|\) and flattens to about 0.07 at \(|\mathbf q|=0.6\), the same along \(\Gamma\)–K and \(\Gamma\)–M; elsewhere the branches move by at most 0.02 (LA at M from 1.360 to 1.381, TA at K from 0.882 to 0.899).
  • On the polar host the coupling block has rank 29 instead of 27, because the long-range forces reach the defect atom. The chain converges as fast as on the non-polar host: errors \(2.3\times10^{-4}\) against \(3.7\times10^{-4}\) (light) and \(5.3\times10^{-5}\) against \(9.5\times10^{-5}\) (heavy) at 120 blocks, and \(10^{-13}\) at 200 blocks, where the chain is close to spanning the \(32\times32\) host.
  • Without the scaling of \(B_0\) the error stops at \(2.0\times10^{-2}\) (light) and \(4.3\times10^{-2}\) (heavy): with long-range forces the scaling is required.

Next

  • A defect that changes the Born charge: its long-range \(\Delta\Phi\) reaches beyond any finite cluster, but is of low rank and can be added to the start block of the chain.

Reproduce

The model is phdef/honeycomb.py. python3 scripts/honeycomb_figures.py --host writes Figure 1 in a few seconds; --defects builds the chain and the exact references and writes the CSV files of Figures 2 and 3 (about 37 minutes on 8 cores; run it on a compute node; HONEYCOMB_CHAIN_CACHE keeps the chain in a file); --plot draws Figures 2 and 3 from the CSV files; --polar runs the polar comparison on a \(32\times32\) mesh (7 minutes on 8 cores) and draws Figures 5 and 6, which --polar-plot redraws from its CSV file; --spectral computes Figure 4 (15 minutes on 8 cores), and examples/honeycomb_spectral_check.py repeats its brute-force check (12 minutes on 16 cores). python -m pytest tests runs the 13 honeycomb tests with the others.
