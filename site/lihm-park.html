Reading notes: the Lihm–Park upfolding

J.-M. Lihm and C.-H. Park, Plasmon–phonon hybridization in doped semiconductors from first principles, arXiv:2409.07393v1 (2024), published in Phys. Rev. Lett. 133, 116402. These notes refer to arXiv v1 and its Supplemental Material (SM); the published version was not compared. Updated 2026-10-01.

The construction was re-derived and every identity below was checked numerically on a toy model; two printed equations need care when implemented.

The construction

The authors never use the word upfolding; the construction is their plasmon–phonon dynamical matrix. Read as the Schur complement used backwards, it has five steps.

1. The self-energy as exchange of an auxiliary boson (SM S8–S11)

With the plasmon-pole dielectric function, the long-range electron–plasmon vertex gq0lr=Ωq0Uqg^{\mathrm{lr}}_{\mathbf q0}=\Omega_{\mathbf q0}\sqrt{U_{\mathbf q}}gq0lr​=Ωq0​Uq​​ and the coupling cqν=gq0lrgqνlr/Uqc_{\mathbf q\nu}=g^{\mathrm{lr}}_{\mathbf q0}g^{\mathrm{lr}}_{\mathbf q\nu}/U_{\mathbf q}cqν​=gq0lr​gqνlr​/Uq​, the phonon self-energy factorizes into a scalar propagator between static couplings:

3. A frequency-independent dynamical matrix (SM S17–S21)

Every diagonal entry has the same form (ω+iγα)2−ωα2(\omega+i\gamma_\alpha)^2-\omega_\alpha^2(ω+iγα​)2−ωα2​ and the couplings are static, so

4. Damping (Eq. 9, SM S22–S42)

Because Γ~\tilde\GammaΓ~ and C~\tilde CC~ do not commute, the quadratic eigenvalue problem is linearized with a position–velocity companion matrix, brought to the form of time-dependent Hartree–Fock (eigenvalues in pairs λ\lambdaλ, −λ∗-\lambda^*−λ∗) and expanded in biorthogonal left and right eigenvectors. The eigenvalues ω~β−iγ~β\tilde\omega_\beta-i\tilde\gamma_\betaω~β​−iγ~​β​ are the energies and linewidths of the damped hybrid modes.

5. Electrons (SM S53–S81)

Electrons couple to the enlarged space through unscreened, static vertices,

W(q,ω)=Uq+∑αα′gmnα D~αα′(q,ω) gmnα′∗(S59),W(\mathbf q,\omega)=U_{\mathbf q}+\sum_{\alpha\alpha'}g_{mn\alpha}\,\tilde D_{\alpha\alpha'}(\mathbf q,\omega)\,g^{*}_{mn\alpha'}\qquad(\mathrm{S59}),W(q,ω)=Uq​+αα′∑​gmnα​D~αα′​(q,ω)gmnα′∗​(S59),

so the frequency-dependent screening of the electron–phonon vertex is absorbed into the off-diagonal blocks of D~\tilde DD~. Rotating the vertices to the hybrid modes (S65, S66) turns the electron self-energy (S70, S71) and the loss function (S78, S81) into sums over modes with the same form as an electron–phonon calculation. The static approximation fits in the same machinery: an auxiliary mode pushed to very high frequency reproduces Π(ω)≈Π(0)\Pi(\omega)\approx\Pi(0)Π(ω)≈Π(0) (S45, S46).

Two notes on the printed equations

Conjugation in S58 and S74

The phonon part of the screened interaction is printed as ∑μνgmnμscr(ω) Dμν(ω) [gmnνscr(ω)]∗\sum_{\mu\nu}g^{\mathrm{scr}}_{mn\mu}(\omega)\,D_{\mu\nu}(\omega)\,[g^{\mathrm{scr}}_{mn\nu}(\omega)]^*∑μν​gmnμscr​(ω)Dμν​(ω)[gmnνscr​(ω)]∗ with gscr=g+gmn0D000(ω) cg^{\mathrm{scr}}=g+g_{mn0}D^0_{00}(\omega)\,cgscr=g+gmn0​D000​(ω)c. The complex conjugation must act on the static vertices only, not on the retarded screening factor D000(ω)D^0_{00}(\omega)D000​(ω): this is how S60–S62 use it, and the upfolded forms S59 and S75 have it built in. Read literally, the expression mixes retarded and advanced functions whenever the plasmon linewidth is finite.

On a toy model with three phonons and one damped plasmon at strong coupling, the upfolded S75 agrees with an independent closed form of the random-phase approximation, Wb/(1−δχ0Wb)W_b/(1-\delta\chi_0W_b)Wb​/(1−δχ0​Wb​), to 4.5×10−154.5\times10^{-15}4.5×10−15. The literal reading of S74 differs by up to 164% at single frequencies, shifts the loss-function peak by 0.4 meV and changes its integrated weight by 5%. To compute the loss function, use S75 or S78.

Pole index in the multipole couplings (S49–S51)

The multipole generalization is printed with couplings cqνc_{\mathbf q\nu}cqν​ that carry no pole index. Each pole needs its own strength,

cpν=Ωˉqp gqνlrUq,c_{p\nu}=\frac{\bar\Omega_{\mathbf qp}\,g^{\mathrm{lr}}_{\mathbf q\nu}}{\sqrt{U_{\mathbf q}}} ,cpν​=Uq​​Ωˉqp​gqνlr​​,

otherwise every pole is forced to the same strength. With two poles of different strength, the pole-resolved couplings reproduce the direct Dyson solution to 7×10−167\times10^{-16}7×10−16; the pole-independent couplings miss it by 1.9×10−21.9\times10^{-2}1.9×10−2.

What we take from it

The move, not the plasmon: write a frequency-dependent self-energy as the exchange of auxiliary modes with static couplings, so that one static matrix replaces a frequency loop and electrons couple through static vertices. For a point defect the auxiliary modes are the sites of a Krylov chain of the host bath rather than a plasmon pole; see the Method.
