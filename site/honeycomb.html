Toy 2: honeycomb lattice

A second toy model with two atoms per cell, like hexagonal boron nitride, and displacements in xxx, yyy and zzz. Updated 2026-10-02.

Why a second toy

The first toy validates the upfolding on a square lattice with one scalar displacement per site. It does not test what the first-principles calculation needs:

  • tensor force constants, 3×33\times33×3 blocks between atoms;
  • two atoms per cell and six branches, with gaps between them;
  • a flexural branch ωZA∝q2\omega_{\mathrm{ZA}}\propto q^2ωZA​∝q2, which exists only if the force constants obey the rotational sum rules;
  • a periodic host sampled on a q\mathbf qq-mesh, as from DFPT, from which the chain can be built in reciprocal space.

Model

A honeycomb lattice with bond length 1, lattice vectors a1=(3,0)\mathbf a_1=(\sqrt3,0)a1​=(3​,0) and a2=(3/2,3/2)\mathbf a_2=(\sqrt3/2,3/2)a2​=(3​/2,3/2), atom A at the origin and atom B at (0,1)(0,1)(0,1). Each atom moves in three directions. The harmonic energy is

E=12∑⟨ij⟩k1[e^ij⋅(uj−ui)]2+12∑⟨⟨ij⟩⟩k2[e^ij⋅(uj−ui)]2+12∑iκ(uiz−13∑j∈nn(i)ujz)2,E=\frac12\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}k_1\big[\hat{\mathbf e}_{ij}\cdot(\mathbf u_j-\mathbf u_i)\big]^2+\frac12\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle}k_2\big[\hat{\mathbf e}_{ij}\cdot(\mathbf u_j-\mathbf u_i)\big]^2+\frac12\sum_i\kappa\Big(u^z_i-\frac13\sum_{j\in\mathrm{nn}(i)}u^z_j\Big)^2,E=21​⟨ij⟩∑​k1​[e^ij​⋅(uj​−ui​)]2+21​⟨⟨ij⟩⟩∑​k2​[e^ij​⋅(uj​−ui​)]2+21​i∑​κ(uiz​−31​j∈nn(i)∑​ujz​)2,

with central springs on the nearest-neighbour A–B bonds (k1k_1k1​) and on the second-neighbour A–A and B–B bonds (k2k_2k2​), and a bending term for every atom that measures how far it sits above or below the plane of its three neighbours. The parameters are mA=1m_A=1mA​=1, mB=1.3m_B=1.3mB​=1.3 (close to the boron-to-nitrogen mass ratio), k1=1k_1=1k1​=1, k2=0.2k_2=0.2k2​=0.2 and κ=0.3\kappa=0.3κ=0.3, chosen so that the ratio of the ZO to the TO frequency at Γ\GammaΓ is 0.63, close to that of h-BN.

  • Sum rules hold exactly. Every term is unchanged by the three rigid translations and the three rigid rotations, so the acoustic and the rotational sum rules hold term by term.
  • The flexural branch is quadratic. A displacement uzu^zuz that is linear in position costs no bending energy, because the three neighbours of an atom are centred on it.
  • In-plane and out-of-plane motion decouple. The mirror plane z→−zz\to-zz→−z splits the 6×66\times66×6 dynamical matrix into a 4×44\times44×4 in-plane block and a 2×22\times22×2 out-of-plane block.
  • Defects. A substitution changes the mass of one atom and multiplies every energy term that contains it by a common factor, so the sum rules still hold exactly and need not be imposed afterwards, unlike force constants spliced from a supercell.

Checks of the host

tests/test_honeycomb.py checks the host in six tests:

  • every energy term annihilates the six rigid motions to 10−1410^{-14}10−14 (acoustic and rotational sum rules);
  • D(q)D(\mathbf q)D(q) is Hermitian, its in-plane and out-of-plane blocks do not couple, the two phase conventions give the same eigenvalues, and there are exactly three zero frequencies at Γ\GammaΓ;
  • ωZA/q2\omega_{\mathrm{ZA}}/q^2ωZA​/q2 and ωTA/q\omega_{\mathrm{TA}}/qωTA​/q are constant for qqq from 0.002 to 0.008, to 10−310^{-3}10−3 and 10−410^{-4}10−4;
  • no negative ω2\omega^2ω2 on a 30×3030\times3030×30 grid;
  • the spectrum of a periodic 5×55\times55×5 supercell equals the Bloch spectrum on the 5×55\times55×5 grid to 10−1210^{-12}10−12;
  • a substitution keeps exactly three zero modes and the uniform translations.

Defects

Both defects replace an A atom, with the same numbers as in the first toy: a light defect, M′=0.3M'=0.3M′=0.3 with every energy term that contains it multiplied by 1.3, and a heavy defect, M′=8M'=8M′=8 with the factor 0.6.

  • Light defect: an in-plane local mode (a doublet) at 3.1551, far above the host top 1.862; an out-of-plane mode at 1.4543 inside the gap 1.360–1.549, with 79 % of its weight on the defect; and a shallow in-plane mode at 1.3773, 0.017 above the top of LA, which has no amplitude on the defect site itself and lives on the neighbours.
  • Heavy defect: an in-plane resonance near 0.26 and a flexural (out-of-plane) resonance near 0.05, both inside the host bands.

Cluster and chain from the Bloch modes

The cluster is the defect and the atoms within two bonds, 10 atoms and 30 coordinates. It contains every term that the substitution changes, and the code asserts that the bath block and the coupling block equal those of the host. The coupling Dbc\mathcal D_{bc}Dbc​ has rank 27: the three coordinates of the defect itself do not couple to the bath.

The chain is built from the host modes of an L×LL\times LL×L q\mathbf qq-mesh, without a real-space bath matrix. With the eigenvectors εν(q)\varepsilon_\nu(\mathbf q)εν​(q) of the cell-gauge D(q)D(\mathbf q)D(q), a cluster coordinate ccc (atom scs_csc​ in cell Rc\mathbf R_cRc​, direction αc\alpha_cαc​) has the components

⟨qν∣ec⟩=1L e−iq⋅Rc εscαc,ν∗(q),\langle\mathbf q\nu|e_c\rangle=\frac1L\,e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_c}\,\varepsilon^{*}_{s_c\alpha_c,\nu}(\mathbf q),⟨qν∣ec​⟩=L1​e−iq⋅Rc​εsc​αc​,ν∗​(q),

and block Lanczos on diag(ωqν2)\mathrm{diag}(\omega^2_{\mathbf q\nu})diag(ωqν2​) started from them gives the cluster block first (equal to W†Dcc0WW^\dagger\mathcal D^0_{cc}WW†Dcc0​W to 10−1410^{-14}10−14) and the chain of the bath after it. This is the reciprocal-space route planned for MoS₂, where DFPT gives the modes on a q\mathbf qq-mesh.

  • Supercell gate. On the same mesh the chain terminates (17 blocks for 8×88\times88×8, 33 for 12×1212\times1212×12) and the upfolded matrix reproduces the defect supercell to 10−1210^{-12}10−12 for the host and both defects; the real-space bath chain gives the same result.
  • One chain for every defect. The chain depends only on the host; each defect changes only the 30×3030\times3030×30 cluster block.

Results on a 64 × 64 mesh

The chain is built from the 6×642=24 5766\times64^2=24\,5766×642=24576 modes of a 64×6464\times6464×64 mesh, 240 blocks of width 27 (26 minutes on 8 cores, dominated by the full reorthogonalization). The reference is the exact 64×6464\times6464×64 defect supercell (sparse direct solves), η=0.02\eta=0.02η=0.02.

  • The upfolded local densities of states pass through every exact point, for both polarizations and both defects (Figure 2).
  • The error of the defect-site Green function falls exponentially, to 5×10−65\times10^{-6}5×10−6 (light, in-plane), 6×10−76\times10^{-7}6×10−7 (light, out-of-plane), 2×10−62\times10^{-6}2×10−6 (heavy, in-plane) and 1×10−71\times10^{-7}1×10−7 (heavy, out-of-plane) with 240 blocks. The out-of-plane sector converges faster because its band is narrower (top 1.03 against 1.86), as the rate ≈4η/ωmax⁡\approx4\eta/\omega_{\max}≈4η/ωmax​ per block of the first toy predicts; the flexural resonance at 0.05 sets no extra difficulty.
  • The deep bound states converge like the local mode of the first toy, by about a factor of 100 per block, to the double-precision floor at six blocks: the in-plane local mode 3.155078073.155078073.15507807 and the out-of-plane mode in the gap 1.454310311.454310311.45431031.
  • The shallow in-plane gap mode, 1.377282961.377282961.37728296, converges like the band edge it sits next to: errors 3.7×10−43.7\times10^{-4}3.7×10−4, 5.1×10−55.1\times10^{-5}5.1×10−5 and 1.9×10−81.9\times10^{-8}1.9×10−8 at 20, 40 and 80 blocks.

Spectral function at a finite concentration

With substitutions on randomly chosen A sites, ndn_dnd​ per cell, the configuration-averaged displacement Green function follows from the T-matrix of one defect, as for the first toy, now as 6×66\times66×6 matrices:

  • Light defect: the host branches barely change. Their weight depends on q\mathbf qq: integrated over the gap, the out-of-plane weight is 0.105 near Γ\GammaΓ, 0.044 at M and 0.040 at K; integrated over 2.6–3.4, the in-plane weight of the local band is 0.110, 0.138 and 0.141.

Polar switch

Hexagonal BN is polar. An option adds in-plane Born charges ±Z\pm Z±Z (A positive, B negative, so they sum to zero) and the dipole–dipole interaction through the screened two-dimensional Coulomb kernel,

Dsα,tβLR(q)=2πZsZtAmsmt∑Gkαkβ∣k∣ (1+reff∣k∣) e−∣k∣2/4λ2 e−iG⋅(τt−τs),k=q+G,D^{\mathrm{LR}}_{s\alpha,t\beta}(\mathbf q)=\frac{2\pi Z_sZ_t}{A\sqrt{m_sm_t}}\sum_{\mathbf G}\frac{k_\alpha k_\beta}{|\mathbf k|\,(1+r_{\mathrm{eff}}|\mathbf k|)}\,e^{-|\mathbf k|^2/4\lambda^2}\,e^{-i\mathbf G\cdot(\boldsymbol\tau_t-\boldsymbol\tau_s)},\qquad\mathbf k=\mathbf q+\mathbf G,Dsα,tβLR​(q)=Ams​mt​​2πZs​Zt​​G∑​∣k∣(1+reff​∣k∣)kα​kβ​​e−∣k∣2/4λ2e−iG⋅(τt​−τs​),k=q+G,

with Z=0.48Z=0.48Z=0.48, reff=2r_{\mathrm{eff}}=2reff​=2 and Gaussian dipoles of width 1/λ1/\lambda1/λ, λ=0.7\lambda=0.7λ=0.7; the q=0\mathbf q=0q=0 row sums are removed on site (acoustic sum rule). In two dimensions LO and TO stay degenerate at Γ\GammaΓ (1.6290) and split linearly away from it, ωLO−ωTO≈0.302 ∣q∣\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}-\omega_{\mathrm{TO}}\approx0.302\,|\mathbf q|ωLO​−ωTO​≈0.302∣q∣; the out-of-plane modes are untouched.

  • A consequence for the chain. With long-range forces the bath couples to the defect atom itself, so in mass-weighted coordinates Dbc\mathcal D_{bc}Dbc​ changes with the defect mass even when the force constants do not. The chain stays a host property: only the columns of B0B_0B0​ scale, by mhost/mdefect\sqrt{m_{\mathrm{host}}/m_{\mathrm{defect}}}mhost​/mdefect​​, which is exact.
  • Tests. Three zero modes and LO = TO at Γ\GammaΓ, linear splitting, mirror symmetry and stability over the zone; the spectrum of a polar 5×55\times55×5 torus equals the Bloch spectrum to 10−1410^{-14}10−14; on the 8×88\times88×8 polar defect supercell the scaled coupling reproduces the exact result to 10−910^{-9}10−9 and the unscaled one does not.
  • The polar term changes the host only near Γ\GammaΓ: the LO–TO splitting starts at 0.302 ∣q∣0.302\,|\mathbf q|0.302∣q∣ and flattens to about 0.07 at ∣q∣=0.6|\mathbf q|=0.6∣q∣=0.6, the same along Γ\GammaΓ–K and Γ\GammaΓ–M; elsewhere the branches move by at most 0.02 (LA at M from 1.360 to 1.381, TA at K from 0.882 to 0.899).
  • On the polar host the coupling block has rank 29 instead of 27, because the long-range forces reach the defect atom. The chain converges as fast as on the non-polar host: errors 2.3×10−42.3\times10^{-4}2.3×10−4 against 3.7×10−43.7\times10^{-4}3.7×10−4 (light) and 5.3×10−55.3\times10^{-5}5.3×10−5 against 9.5×10−59.5\times10^{-5}9.5×10−5 (heavy) at 120 blocks, and 10−1310^{-13}10−13 at 200 blocks, where the chain is close to spanning the 32×3232\times3232×32 host.
  • Without the scaling of B0B_0B0​ the error stops at 2.0×10−22.0\times10^{-2}2.0×10−2 (light) and 4.3×10−24.3\times10^{-2}4.3×10−2 (heavy): with long-range forces the scaling is required.

Reproduce

The model is phdef/honeycomb.py. python3 scripts/honeycomb_figures.py --host writes Figure 1 in a few seconds; --defects builds the chain and the exact references and writes the CSV files of Figures 2 and 3 (about 37 minutes on 8 cores; run it on a compute node; HONEYCOMB_CHAIN_CACHE keeps the chain in a file); --plot draws Figures 2 and 3 from the CSV files; --polar runs the polar comparison on a 32×3232\times3232×32 mesh (7 minutes on 8 cores) and draws Figures 5 and 6, which --polar-plot redraws from its CSV file; --spectral computes Figure 4 (15 minutes on 8 cores), and examples/honeycomb_spectral_check.py repeats its brute-force check (12 minutes on 16 cores). python -m pytest tests runs the 13 honeycomb tests with the others.
