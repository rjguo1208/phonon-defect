Plan: MoS₂ with OS and VS

First first-principles application: monolayer MoS₂ with an oxygen substitution (OS) or a sulfur vacancy (VS) at nd=1012 cm−2n_d=10^{12}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}}nd​=1012cm−2. Updated 2026-10-01.

Why these two defects

  • OS: a light atom on stiffer bonds. The archived phonon T-matrix finds true local modes above the host spectrum (top 56.8 meV): an e doublet at 59.2 meV and an a1 mode at 66.2 meV, confirmed by direct supercell diagonalization to 0.12–0.15 meV. On the electron side OS is isovalent, with a scattering resonance near the conduction-band edge. Both ingredients of a resonance-enhanced cross term are present.
  • VS: no local modes, but in-band phonon resonances (a1 40.9 meV, e 42.2 and 46.7 meV) and deep electronic gap states. It tests the in-band part of the chain and the treatment of a removed atom.

Steps

  1. P0, host. Primitive-cell DFPT with the 2D Coulomb treatment, interatomic force constants with rotational sum rules for the flexural branch; Wannier electron–phonon matrix elements (EPW) for the host vertex and the bulk electron–phonon rates Γkph\Gamma^{\mathrm{ph}}_{\mathbf k}Γkph​.
  2. P1, defect cluster. Relaxed defect supercell; symmetric displacements ±δ\pm\delta±δ with δ\deltaδ of about 0.01–0.02 Å for every cluster atom (defect plus neighbour shells, reduced by the C3vC_{3v}C3v​ symmetry). Keep both the forces and the self-consistent potentials of every displaced calculation.
  3. P2, cluster block. Splice ΔΦ\Delta\PhiΔΦ into the host force constants, re-impose the acoustic sum rule, weight by the defect masses: Dcc\mathcal D_{cc}Dcc​.
  4. P3, chain. Block Lanczos on the host bath from Dbc\mathcal D_{bc}Dbc​, or the Stieltjes recursion on the cached cluster spectral density if the archived pipeline output is still available.
  5. P4, upfolded phonons. C~\tilde CC~ and C~0\tilde C_0C~0​; local and resonant modes; gates U1–U3 and U7.
  6. P5, vertices. EDI matrix elements of the finite-difference potentials for the cluster coordinates; host vertex projected on the chain sites; rotation to the dressed modes; gate U4.
  7. P6, electrons. Scattering states Ψ±\Psi^{\pm}Ψ± from the downfolded electron–defect T-matrix; distorted-wave amplitudes; ΔΓk\Delta\Gamma_{\mathbf k}ΔΓk​; gates U5, U6 and U8.
  8. P7, transport. Energy-coupled Boltzmann equation with elastic and inelastic kernels; mobility versus temperature; comparison with Matthiessen addition.

Cost

A rough estimate, to be refined after P0. The archived pipeline needed about 13 node-hours for VS and 12 for OS, most of it in about 125 finite displacements per defect. Here the displacements are limited to the cluster but each needs its potential and one matrix-element run as well as its forces:

Risks

  • Splicing supercell force constants into the host breaks the acoustic sum rule unless it is re-imposed on the cluster atoms.
  • A 6×66\times66×6 supercell is about 19 Å across; force constants and potentials near its boundary contain periodic images. Keep the cluster well inside and use host values for the cluster–bath coupling.
  • A vacancy removes three coordinates, which the pristine reference C~0\tilde C_0C~0​ still contains; the subtraction must account for them.
  • Finite-difference potentials must share one potential alignment, or a constant leaks into the vertex.
  • The flexural ZA branch needs a long chain at low frequency.
  • If thermal carriers reach a defect resonance, nonadiabatic effects enter (Method).
