Method: upfolding the defect–phonon problem

How the phonons of a crystal with a point defect are written as one static, frequency-independent matrix, how electrons couple to its modes, and how the result enters electron transport. Updated 2026-10-01.

Design document. The phonon upfolding is validated on a toy lattice; the electron side has not been implemented.

Scope and order

  • Harmonic lattice; point defects at concentration nd→0n_d\to0nd​→0 (defects per primitive cell), keeping terms of order ndn_dnd​.
  • Defect potential to all orders (electron–defect T-matrix); electron–phonon coupling to second order with bare vertices (Migdal).
  • Adiabatic phonons from finite displacements; the nonadiabatic correction is treated in its own section.
  • Carriers near band edges or a Fermi surface; transport needs on-shell rates. Units with ℏ=1\hbar=1ℏ=1.

What differs from the plasmon–phonon case

In the plasmon–phonon case of Lihm and Park the phonon self-energy is Πμν(ω)=cqμ∗D000(ω) cqν\Pi_{\mu\nu}(\omega)=c^{*}_{\mathbf q\mu}D^0_{00}(\omega)\,c_{\mathbf q\nu}Πμν​(ω)=cqμ∗​D000​(ω)cqν​: its frequency dependence comes from an external response, the plasmon, and translation symmetry reduces the problem to one (Nph+1)(N_{\mathrm{ph}}+1)(Nph​+1)-dimensional matrix per q\mathbf qq. A point defect breaks translation symmetry, and its harmonic phonons are static. With the defect masses MMM and force constants Φ=Φ0+ΔΦ\Phi=\Phi^0+\Delta\PhiΦ=Φ0+ΔΦ,

[ω2−D]x=0,D=M−1/2 Φ M−1/2,x=M1/2u.\big[\omega^2-\mathcal D\big]x=0,\qquad \mathcal D=M^{-1/2}\,\Phi\,M^{-1/2},\qquad x=M^{1/2}u .[ω2−D]x=0,D=M−1/2ΦM−1/2,x=M1/2u.

There is no external pole to add. Frequency dependence appears only after the infinite host is folded onto a finite cluster around the defect, so the object to upfold is the host phonon bath.

Exact downfold onto the defect cluster

Split the mass-weighted displacement coordinates into a cluster ccc and the host bath bbb. The cluster contains the defect, every atom whose mass or force constants change, and every atom whose electron–phonon vertex changes. Because every change is inside the cluster, Dbb\mathcal D_{bb}Dbb​ and Dbc\mathcal D_{bc}Dbc​ are pure host quantities. Eliminating the bath exactly,

Dcc(z)=[z−Dcc−Πb(z)]−1,Πb(z)=Dcb (z−Dbb)−1Dbc,z=(ω+i0+)2.D_{cc}(z)=\big[z-\mathcal D_{cc}-\Pi_b(z)\big]^{-1},\qquad \Pi_b(z)=\mathcal D_{cb}\,\big(z-\mathcal D_{bb}\big)^{-1}\mathcal D_{bc},\qquad z=(\omega+i0^+)^2 .Dcc​(z)=[z−Dcc​−Πb​(z)]−1,Πb​(z)=Dcb​(z−Dbb​)−1Dbc​,z=(ω+i0+)2.

Πb\Pi_bΠb​ is the phonon counterpart of the rest-space self-energy of an electronic downfolding: exact, frequency dependent, with poles at the eigenvalues of the bath.

where E1E_1E1​ places a block on the first chain site. This is the analogue of the plasmon–phonon dynamical matrix of Lihm and Park (SM Eq. S18): the cluster plays the role of the phonons, the chain sites the role of the plasmon, and B0B_0B0​ the role of the couplings cqνc_{\mathbf q\nu}cqν​.

  • The chain does not depend on the defect. It depends only on Dbb\mathcal D_{bb}Dbb​ and Dbc\mathcal D_{bc}Dbc​. It is built once per host and cluster and reused for every defect on the same site; a different defect changes only the block Dcc\mathcal D_{cc}Dcc​. The pristine reference C~0\tilde C_0C~0​ uses the host block Dcc0\mathcal D^0_{cc}Dcc0​ with the same chain.
  • One diagonalization gives every frequency. With C~eβ=ω~β2eβ\tilde C e_\beta=\tilde\omega_\beta^2e_\betaC~eβ​=ω~β2​eβ​, the cluster propagator is a sum over defect-dressed modes,
    Dcc(z)=∑βecβ ecβ†z−ω~β2.D_{cc}(z)=\sum_\beta\frac{e_{c\beta}\,e_{c\beta}^{\dagger}}{z-\tilde\omega_\beta^2} .Dcc​(z)=β∑​z−ω~β2​ecβ​ecβ†​​.
  • Convergence. Local modes outside the host spectrum are bound states and converge exponentially with mmm. In-band resonances need a chain that resolves the bath at the chosen broadening, roughly m∼m\simm∼ bandwidth / η\etaη. On the toy lattice 160 blocks give a relative error of 5×10−65\times10^{-6}5×10−6 at η=0.05\eta=0.05η=0.05, with the band top at 222\sqrt222​.
  • Without diagonalization. The block continued fraction gives the same Dcc(z)D_{cc}(z)Dcc​(z) by one small recursion per frequency; on the toy lattice the two agree to 10−1310^{-13}10−13.

Continuum and damping

A finite chain discretizes the host continuum. There are three ways to treat it:

  1. a long chain and a broadening η\etaη;
  2. the block continued fraction, evaluated per frequency;
  3. a constant damping Γ\GammaΓ on the last chain block, which turns z−C~z-\tilde Cz−C~ into (ω+iΓ~)2−C~(\omega+i\tilde\Gamma)^2-\tilde C(ω+iΓ~)2−C~.

Option 3 is linearized exactly as in Eq. 9 of Lihm and Park:

K=(−iΓ~IC~−iΓ~),[(ω+iΓ~)2−C~]−1=(I0)(ω−K)−1(0I).K=\begin{pmatrix}-i\tilde\Gamma&I\\ \tilde C&-i\tilde\Gamma\end{pmatrix},\qquad \big[(\omega+i\tilde\Gamma)^2-\tilde C\big]^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\end{pmatrix}(\omega-K)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0\\ I\end{pmatrix} .K=(−iΓ~C~​I−iΓ~​),[(ω+iΓ~)2−C~]−1=(I​0​)(ω−K)−1(0I​).

The eigenvalues ω~β−iγ~β\tilde\omega_\beta-i\tilde\gamma_\betaω~β​−iγ~​β​ of KKK give the energies and lifetimes of resonant modes. A constant damping is a Markovian terminator and therefore an approximation; options 1 and 2 converge to the exact cluster resolvent.

Static electron–phonon vertices in the upfolded basis

Electrons couple linearly to the mass-weighted displacements xax_axa​ through

Cluster coordinates

Use symmetric finite differences of defect-supercell potentials,

∂VKS∂ua≈VKS(ua=+δ)−VKS(ua=−δ)2δ.\frac{\partial V_{\mathrm{KS}}}{\partial u_a}\approx\frac{V_{\mathrm{KS}}(u_a=+\delta)-V_{\mathrm{KS}}(u_a=-\delta)}{2\delta} .∂ua​∂VKS​​≈2δVKS​(ua​=+δ)−VKS​(ua​=−δ)​.

Matrix elements of a supercell potential difference between host Bloch states are exactly what an electron–defect matrix-element code evaluates (here EDI), so no new matrix-element code is needed. The displaced self-consistent calculations give both the forces, hence the cluster force constants, and the potentials, hence the cluster vertices. The vertex includes the change of the defect potential itself. The displacement must be small and symmetric (about 0.01–0.02 Å), and both potentials must use the same alignment.

Chain coordinates

Contract the real-space host vertex of a Wannier electron–phonon code (EPW) with the Krylov vectors,

gmn,j=∑a∈bgmn,ahost (Qj)a,g_{mn,j}=\sum_{a\in b}g^{\mathrm{host}}_{mn,a}\,(Q_j)_a ,gmn,j​=a∈b∑​gmn,ahost​(Qj​)a​,

storing the QjQ_jQj​ or regenerating them in a second Lanczos pass. A first version keeps only the cluster vertices and converges the result in the cluster radius.

is SM Eq. S66 of Lihm and Park with the hybrid eigenvectors replaced by the dressed defect modes. The defect contribution is the difference of two mode sums, one over C~\tilde CC~ and one over C~0\tilde C_0C~0​, with the same chain and the same chain vertices. Host modes outside the Krylov space generated from Dbc\mathcal D_{bc}Dbc​ neither couple to the cluster nor change with the defect, so they cancel exactly in the difference.

Transport and the rest space

  • Inelastic rates couple different energies, so the Boltzmann equation is solved with an energy-coupled kernel, as in electron–phonon transport, with the elastic defect kernel added.
  • Only on-shell quantities enter transport. Intermediate electron energies lie within the phonon bandwidth of the carrier energy and therefore inside the active window, so no upper-Fan sum over the rest space is needed.
  • The vertex between dressed states may still need the rest space. For a bound state at real energy EEE with ⟨ΨA∣ΨA⟩=1\langle\Psi_A|\Psi_A\rangle=1⟨ΨA​∣ΨA​⟩=1, the active-space weight is

    which needs no rest-space vectors. If ZAZ_AZA​ is well below 1, use the downfolded vertex
    gAAeff(z′,z)=gAA+gAR U(z)+U(z′)†gRA+U(z′)†gRR U(z),g^{\mathrm{eff}}_{AA}(z',z)=g_{AA}+g_{AR}\,\mathcal U(z)+\mathcal U(z')^{\dagger}g_{RA}+\mathcal U(z')^{\dagger}g_{RR}\,\mathcal U(z),gAAeff​(z′,z)=gAA​+gAR​U(z)+U(z′)†gRA​+U(z′)†gRR​U(z),
    which uses the same block-Lanczos machinery as the rest-space self-energy.

Nonadiabatic extension

Finite-displacement force constants are adiabatic. When electronic excitations localized at the defect fall inside the phonon window (free carriers excited into a shallow resonance, a defect level at the Fermi energy of a metal, or the free-carrier plasmon of a doped semiconductor), the phonons acquire a nonadiabatic self-energy. Its poles are added to C~\tilde CC~ as auxiliary modes exactly as in SM Eq. S50 of Lihm and Park, with pole-resolved couplings cpνc_{p\nu}cpν​ (see the Plasmon–phonon notes). The first version is adiabatic.

Relation to the host-mass form

The earlier phonon T-matrix of this repository (tag archive/2026-07-01) scaled the equations of motion by the host masses, which makes a mass change a frequency-dependent perturbation,

[z(I+ε)−D0−ΔD]u~=0,V(z)=ΔD−z ε.\big[z(I+\varepsilon)-\mathcal D_0-\Delta\mathcal D\big]\tilde u=0,\qquad V(z)=\Delta\mathcal D-z\,\varepsilon .[z(I+ε)−D0​−ΔD]u~=0,V(z)=ΔD−zε.

Rescaling the cluster coordinates by (I+ε)−1/2(I+\varepsilon)^{-1/2}(I+ε)−1/2 turns this into the static defect-mass form used here; both give the same modes, but the upfolded matrix needs the static form. When the atoms whose mass changes do not couple directly to the bath, the coupling block Dbc\mathcal D_{bc}Dbc​ is unchanged by the rescaling.

The cluster spectral density ρab(λ)\rho_{ab}(\lambda)ρab​(λ) cached by that pipeline is the matrix-valued spectral measure of the host restricted to the cluster. A block Lanczos (Stieltjes) recursion on that measure generates the same chain, with first block A1=Dcc0A_1=\mathcal D^0_{cc}A1​=Dcc0​, without touching the bulk matrix.
